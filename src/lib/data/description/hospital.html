This is a hospital management system for a private clinic. I rebuilt the parts that deal with money: admissions, bills, medicine, advance payments, and the accounting that has to balance behind all of them.

It started as an off-the-shelf CodeIgniter hospital system with a public website, doctors, departments, appointments, beds, pharmacy, and an accounts module. The clinic's real process didn't match it. Patients are admitted, given services and medicine over several days, pay in advance in parts, are discharged, and the doctors, staff, and referring agents who were involved all have to be paid. Over a couple of months, and a little over a hundred commits, I reworked that whole flow in PHP, MySQL, and jQuery, with mPDF for printed documents.

A bill is the easy part. The hard part is that every line on it is also a journal entry somewhere else.

From admission to discharge

I rebuilt admission as one guided form. It registers or looks up the patient, assigns a bed, records the referring doctor and agent, adds an optional admission fee, and takes an advance payment with its own printed receipt. While the patient is in, services, medicines, and bed days build up on a single bill for that admission. Discharge closes everything in one step: it works out the final bed charge, prompts for any balance still owed, frees the bed, and prints a separate discharge summary and bill. A lot of the commits are edge cases in this flow, like editing an admission after discharge, a bed changing mid-stay, or advance payments dated before the bill.

Services that carry their own costs

A service like a surgery isn't one price. It comes with medicines and with people: a surgeon, an anaesthetist, assistants. I changed services and packages so they can define a bundle of medicines and a list of staff roles with what each is paid. When the service is added to a bill, the medicines are taken from pharmacy stock and listed under that service, and the staff lines are filled in ready to assign. Prices and discounts can still be changed per bill, because the front desk always needs to.

Bookkeeping without the bookkeeper

The accounts module uses double-entry vouchers and a chart of accounts. Before, someone had to enter every voucher by hand after the fact. Now saving a bill creates them automatically. Payments become credit vouchers against cash or card. Each staff member assigned to a service gets a payable voucher with a narration that names the patient, the admission, the service, and their role. Referral agents get their commission the same way. Doctors, staff, and agents get their own account heads, created the first time they're needed, so each person's balance shows up in the general ledger without anyone setting it up. Voucher numbers are given out in sequence, and editing a bill updates its vouchers instead of duplicating them.

On the reporting side I fixed the cash book and general ledger calculations, added narration to the cash book view, added a voucher reject step to approvals, and rewrote the summary reports so people outside accounts can read them.

At a glance

  • — Admission to discharge as one connected flow
  • — Advance payments with dated receipts
  • — Service bundles of medicines and staff roles
  • — Pharmacy stock drawn down from bills
  • — Automatic vouchers for payments, staff, and agents
  • — Account heads created per doctor, employee, and agent
  • — Cash book and ledger fixes and readable reports
  • — Printed documents: receipts, bills, discharge summaries

Working inside someone else's older codebase meant fitting in with how it already did things, not rewriting it. Most of the value came from listening to how the front desk actually worked and making the software match.
